It usually hits in the hotel lift, around nine at night: your legs are done, and the step counter reads 24,000. Macau is a city you cover on foot — the old-town streets on the peninsula, the narrow lanes of Taipa, then Cotai’s night views and malls — and a single day here can out-walk a normal week. Almost nobody budgets for that in advance. The smart move isn’t collapsing into bed; it’s reserving the last slot of the day for a sauna, so your body actually unwinds before you sleep. Five principles cover it.

## Let the End of Your Route Pick the Venue

Picture standing outside a Cotai resort at 8 pm, feet aching, about to ride twenty minutes back across the water because you booked the wrong side of town. That’s the classic mistake. The rule is simple: finish where your route ends and your hotel sits — never backtrack across districts. Ending on the peninsula, around the old town and city centre? Look at [Empire](/en/spa/empire-sauna/), [Number Nine](/en/spa/number-nine-sauna/) in the Royal Dragon Hotel, [Majesty](/en/spa/majesty-spa/), or [The Excellent](/en/spa/the-excellent-sauna/) inside the Grand Emperor Hotel’s main building. Ending in Taipa or Cotai? [Manhao](/en/spa/manhao-spa/) sits in Taipa’s Grandview Hotel and [Shang Pin](/en/spa/shang-pin-spa/) inside the Lisboeta. If your route ends in one district and your hotel is in the other, side with the hotel — the ride after you leave the venue is the leg of the day you’ll feel most. For how the six venues differ, start with the [six-venue guide](/en/blog/macau-sauna-six-venues-guide/).

## Sort Your Belongings Before You Walk In

Two shopping bags in hand and a phone at 12% is no way to enter — you’ll be in the bath with your mind still on your stuff. Settle three things at the door. Storage: every venue has lockers, and larger hauls can be checked in, so there’s no need to detour back to the hotel first. Charging: most venues let you top up the phone during the lounge stretch, so don’t carry battery anxiety inside. Clothes: robes and changing facilities are provided, but after a full day on your feet, a fresh dry top for the ride home is a small luxury worth packing. And one counterintuitive bonus: the few hours your phone spends locked away are often the only genuinely offline rest of the whole trip.

## Dinner First, or Sauna First

Half past seven, and you’ve already worked through half the snack stalls on Rua do Cunha — so which comes first, the proper meal or the sauna? Both orders work; pick by temperament. Dinner first: keep it light, since a full stomach and an immediate soak-and-massage don’t mix, but you enter with the whole evening free of clock-watching. Sauna first: better for people who want a long, slow session, with a late supper after — neither the peninsula nor Cotai runs short of midnight food. Pressed for time, there’s a third option: the venues serve food in-house. Check the clocks too: [The Excellent](/en/spa/the-excellent-sauna/) is open 24 hours, with therapists on duty from 13:00 to 05:00 and overnight stays available in its 24-hour rest area; Manhao runs until 4 am but doesn’t take overnight stays; Empire, Shang Pin, Number Nine and Majesty are also open 24 hours, so however late dinner runs, they’re open.

## Give a Pickup Point a Driver Can Find

“I’m at the Ruins of St. Paul’s” is not a pickup point — the area around the facade is pedestrianised and cars can’t reach it. Tourist-spot map pins are vague at the best of times, and the old town is a maze of one-way streets. When you book the car, name somewhere a vehicle can actually stop: a nearby hotel lobby entrance, or the front door of a landmark building. ## Get Your Group on the Same Clock

By evening, someone always wants one more street and someone else just wants to lie flat. Travelling as a group, settle three things before you set out. A stop time: fix an hour after which nobody proposes “one more block”. A budget: agree the venue spend plus travel time in advance — entry sits around MOP 1,000, mainstream service tiers roughly 2,500–4,500, always subject to the day’s quoted prices. A rough leaving time: it makes booking the return car painless. Once inside, the group can actually split up — the lounges are built for people moving at different speeds — as long as you’ve fixed a meeting time.
