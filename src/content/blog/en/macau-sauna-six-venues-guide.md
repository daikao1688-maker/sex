Macau’s sauna scene went through a shake-up heading into 2026, and the venues genuinely worth knowing now come down to six: Empire, Manhao, Shang Pin, Number Nine, Majesty and The Excellent. “Which one is best?” is the question we hear most, and the honest answer is that there isn’t one — the six have very different personalities. Some guests are all about the rooms, some come purely for the catwalk shows, and some only care whether they can stay until morning. This guide profiles each venue one by one: what its signature is, and who it suits. Start with the overview table, then dig into the details.

| Venue | Location | Signature | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Empire Sauna | Macau Peninsula | Themed suites + water beds, no service charge | Guests who judge a venue by its rooms |
| Manhao Spa | Grandview Hotel, Taipa | Macau’s biggest catwalk selection hall | Cotai-based guests who want the show |
| Shang Pin Spa | Lisboeta Macau, Cotai | 24-hour opening, Japanese bubble bath | Overnighters and Cotai hotel guests |
| Number Nine Spa | Royal Dragon Hotel, Peninsula | Fashion-show stage + scenario-themed rooms | Fans of stage atmosphere and JP/KR-style tiers |
| Majesty Spa | Macau Peninsula | 10+ themed rooms + KTV party rooms | Group parties and themed-room enthusiasts |
| The Excellent Sauna | Grand Emperor Hotel, Peninsula | 24-hour opening + scenario-room upgrades | Flexible visits and overnight rest |

Pricing is structured similarly across all six: entry starts around MOP 1,000, mainstream service tiers run roughly 2,500–4,500, and the top Japanese/Korean tiers mostly start from 5,000 — always confirmed against the day’s quote. ## Empire Sauna: the newest opening, built around its suites

[Empire Sauna](/en/spa/empire-sauna/) is currently Macau’s newest venue, opened in mid-May 2026 with a modern-luxury-meets-oriental-zen design. Its bet is squarely on the rooms themselves: hotel-grade themed suites, marble baths and a signature water-bed room, backed by butler-style service — the experience lands closer to a night in a boutique hotel than a classic sauna. It runs 24 hours, allows overnight stays, and charges no 10% service fee; booking through us adds two free add-on services. If brand-new hardware is your priority, start here.

## Manhao Spa: Macau’s biggest catwalk hall

Staying in Taipa or Cotai, [Manhao Spa](/en/spa/manhao-spa/) is the natural first look — it sits in the Grandview Hotel, about five minutes by car from the heart of Cotai. Its signature boils down to one word: the show. The catwalk selection hall is the largest in Macau — several open tiers rising through a wash of cool blue light — and comes closer to a staged performance than anywhere else in town, while the marble lobby and mural corridors carry the “regal” theme throughout. Rooms are integrated suites that combine a bedroom with a water bed, headlined by the signature red water bed under a spotlight. Note the hours — 14:00–04:00, with no overnight stays at present; booking through us adds two free add-on services (one if you choose the Shanghai-style massage).

## Shang Pin Spa: the answer for overnighters and Cotai guests

[Shang Pin Spa](/en/spa/shang-pin-spa/) operates inside the Lisboeta in Cotai, with an ornate, high-end interior and modern facilities. Its real strength is time: open 24 hours with overnight stays allowed, and lounge recliners you can sleep on until morning — genuinely useful if the night runs long and you’d rather not book another hotel room. The house specialty is the Japanese bubble bath, and package promotions rotate periodically — ask on the day for what’s running. If you’re staying at the Lisboeta or anywhere in Cotai, it’s the lowest-friction choice of the six.

## Number Nine Spa: a fashion show moved indoors

[Number Nine Spa](/en/spa/number-nine-sauna/) sits in the Royal Dragon Hotel in the city centre and opened in April 2026 — the first of this new wave to launch. There’s a fashion-show stage with an LED screen inside, so choosing a technician plays out like watching a runway; the rooms are scenario-themed, with deliberate lighting and set design. Its menu leads with the Japanese/Korean tiers, it runs 24 hours with overnight stays, and booking through us adds two free add-on services. If stage atmosphere and the JP/KR route are your thing, this is the closest match.

## Majesty Spa: themed rooms plus KTV, the party venue

[Majesty Spa](/en/spa/majesty-spa/) has arguably the most lavish interior in Macau, and its hall uses an up-close “scenery” selection format. It also owns a combination no other venue offers: more than ten elaborately built themed rooms — Egyptian, Chinese wedding-bed, gym-and-boxing, wedding and cosplay themes among them — plus KTV party rooms, a rarity in Macau. A group booking a room to sing first and then splitting off for their own plans is a play unique to this house. No service charge, open 24 hours, overnight stays allowed.

## The Excellent Sauna: 24-hour opening, small and curated

[The Excellent Sauna](/en/spa/the-excellent-sauna/) is in the Grand Emperor Hotel’s main building and is open 24 hours, with therapists on duty from 13:00 to 05:00. It leads with newly built Japanese-theme rooms, and scenario rooms — DJ booth, office, cinema — are available as paid add-ons, which keeps the format flexible. It’s a smaller house that deliberately runs a short, curated menu. After the session, guests can stay overnight in the 24-hour rest area, so there is no need to leave before morning.
